# "Sino Ba Talaga ang Nag-imbento ng Adobong Manok?" — Tagalog script v1 (2026-11-23)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (~62-66 s), Tagalog, ElevenLabs eleven_v4 voice (steady documentary storyteller tone), no captions or labels.
Facts:
- Philippine adobo is an indigenous pre-colonial Filipino cooking method, older than Ferdinand Magellan's arrival in 1521. Before refrigeration existed in the hot, humid tropical archipelago, early Filipinos preserved chicken, pork, and seafood by simmering them in clay pots (palayok) with native vinegar (cane or palm sap), garlic, and salt.
- Pre-colonial adobo had no soy sauce and was clear/white ("adobong puti"). Because vinegar's acetic acid inhibits bacterial growth and tenderizes meat fibers, it kept food safe for days even without ice or cooling.
- The Spanish word "adobo" (from "adobar", meaning to marinate or pickle) was first applied to the native Filipino dish in 1613 by Franciscan missionary Fray Pedro de San Buenaventura in his Tagalog-Spanish dictionary *Vocabulario de la Lengua Tagala*, where he called it "adobo de los naturales" ("adobo of the natives"). The original indigenous Tagalog name of the dish was never recorded and was lost to history.
- Later, through trade with Chinese merchants, soy sauce (toyo) entered Filipino kitchens and replaced salt in many regions, giving modern chicken adobo its signature dark, savory glaze.
- Because Philippine adobo is a cooking method rather than one fixed recipe, regional styles flourished across the islands: Adobong Puti (clear vinegar/garlic), Adobo sa Gata (Bicol/southern Philippines with coconut milk), and Adobo sa Dilaw (Batangas with turmeric).
- In 2021, the Philippine DTI Bureau of Philippine Standards announced a project to draft a technical standard for adobo, sparking affectionate nationwide debate because every Filipino family insists their own home-cooked adobo is the best.
- Everyday analogy: Adobo works like a kitchen-made refrigerator — instead of ice, vinegar and salt act as an edible shield against spoilage.
(Sources: Britannica "Adobo"; Ambeth Ocampo; Doreen Fernandez & Edilberto Alegre, *Kinilaw* (1991); Pedro de San Buenaventura, *Vocabulario de la Lengua Tagala* (1613); Wikipedia "Adobo (Filipino dish)")
